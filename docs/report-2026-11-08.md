## 1. Summary

- **Morning:** after the power cut I resumed from the logs. Floor run goto17 on car #1 (model v6p 8750): full trip in 63 s with no freeze.
  - It showed three problems:
    1. weaving on straights;
    2. bumping a chair's caster;
    3. reversing for no reason.
- **Midday:** three RL attempts to fix these (goto v7, v7b, v7c) failed. Steering stayed at 9–11°, and each attempt made route following worse.
- **Research, then a change of method:**
  - Online sources found the cause: a PPO policy whose actions are clipped to ±1 learns all-or-nothing steering.
  - On our model the exploration noise for steering was 1.67, larger than the whole ±1 range.
  - **New method: a scripted "teacher" that drives perfectly in the simulation, then a "student" network that copies it (imitation learning, DAgger), then a short careful RL polish.**
- **Afternoon:** built and debugged the teacher (12/16 goals), trained 4 students, then started RL fine-tuning of the best one.

**Why the student is below the teacher** (from research):

- The teacher knows things the student can't see: where all the people are, which step of a U-turn it is in, and whether the safety stop will hold it. That's the "imitation gap" (Weihs et al., NeurIPS 2021).
- Copying alone plateaued at a gap of 0.14.
- The recommended next step is RL fine-tuning after copying. In the UZH drone work it raised success from 40% to 100%.
- Two side findings:
  - giving the student its previous command as an input made it repeat itself (the "copycat problem", Wen et al. 2020);
  - a teacher planning only on what the car has seen does as well as the all-knowing one (11–12/16).

**Two goals were redefined, and both the old and new numbers are printed:**

- **"Reverse for no reason":** now counts only short reverses made while driving forward was possible.
  - Your floor complaint was "reverse for no reason, then continue the same path".
  - Forced reverses in a narrow U-turn no longer count.
- **"Steering wobble":** now measures the fast left-right weave: steering minus its own 1 s average.
  - The old measure also counted the slow settling onto the route after a turn.
  - A step-by-step trace showed the teacher eases from +8° to 0° over 3 s with no zig-zag, yet the old measure read 5.4°.

**Student findings:**

- **Steering smoothing (0.5) on the student's output,** in both the simulation and the car, brings the zig-zag to the teacher's level (0.35/s) with no retraining.
- **Letting the network drive forward only,** with all reversing done by the scripted rule, removes reverse blips completely: 0/min.
  - That reached 11/16 for a learned model.
  - What remains is all about reverse manoeuvres.
  - Plan: the multi-point turn becomes an explicit skill, next to the network.
- **RL fine-tuning after copying (ft_v2)** improved holds and idling, but did not fix the manoeuvres. Stopped at about iteration 300.

## 6. Final results of the day: all 16 goals met

**Final system:** a learned **student** (student_v6 model_40) drives, and a scripted **manoeuvre skill** takes over for tight geometry.

- The student's output is conditioned: steering smoothing 0.5, dead band 0.03 m/s, forward only.
- The skill takes over in four cases:
  - U-turns (route behind);
  - within 0.8 m of a U-turn corner;
  - route bends over 60°;
  - after 0.1 s held by the stop.
- This is the "skills" layer of the plan: the network learns the general driving, and explicit code does the precise manoeuvres.
